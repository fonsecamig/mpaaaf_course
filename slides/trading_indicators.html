Returns & Volatility

Log-Return

\[r_t(n) = \ln\left(\frac{C_t}{C_{t-n}}\right)\]

Motivation: Log-returns are symmetric, additive over time, and approximately normally distributed — the standard unit of measurement in quantitative finance.

Interpretation:

  • \(r > 0\) appreciation
  • \(r < 0\) depreciation.

Typical periods: \(n = 1, 5, 10, 20\).

Realised Volatility

\[\sigma_n = \text{std}\!\left(\{r_{t-n+1}, \ldots, r_t\}\right)\]

Motivation: Rolling standard deviation of log-returns is the most direct empirical measure of price uncertainty. Captures regime changes between calm and turbulent markets.

Interpretation:

  • Low = calm/trending regime
  • rising = growing uncertainty
  • high = crisis or event-driven.

Typical periods: \(n = 5, 10, 20, 60\).

Overnight Gap

\[\text{Gap}_t = \ln\!\left(\frac{O_t}{C_{t-1}}\right)\]

Motivation: News, earnings, and macro events accumulate after hours. The overnight gap captures the information shock that arrives between sessions and cannot be hedged intraday.

Interpretation:

  • \(> 0\) gap up (positive overnight news)
  • \(< 0\) gap down (negative news).

Persistent gaps in one direction signal trend continuation.

Intraday Range

\[\text{Range}_t = \frac{H_t - L_t}{C_t}\]

Motivation: The High-Low spread normalised by Close measures intraday uncertainty independent of price level. Reacts to news within the same session, before close-to-close volatility can update.

Interpretation:

  • Low = quiet session
  • spike = news or event
  • persistently high = volatile regime.

No lookback parameter — uses the current bar only.

ATR — Average True Range

\[\text{TR}_t = \max(H_t - L_t,\ |H_t - C_{t-1}|,\ |L_t - C_{t-1}|)\] \[\text{ATR}(n) = \text{mean}(\text{TR},\, n) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \text{TR}_{t-n+i}\]

Motivation: Extends the simple H-L range to account for overnight gaps. The standard input for volatility-scaled position sizing and stop-loss placement.

Interpretation:

  • Rising = volatility expanding
  • falling = contracting.

Common stop rule: \(k \times \text{ATR}\) with \(k = 1.5\)–\(3\). Typical periods: \(n = 7, 14, 21\).

Garman-Klass / Parkinson Estimator

\[\hat{\sigma}^2_{GK} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\ln^2\!\frac{H_i}{L_i} - (2\ln 2 - 1)\ln^2\!\frac{C_i}{O_i}\right]\]

Motivation: Close-to-close volatility discards intraday information. The Garman-Klass estimator uses the full OHLC bar, achieving 5–8× lower variance than the standard estimator for the same window.

Interpretation: Same scale as realised volatility. Preferred for short windows or when intraday data is unavailable. Typical periods: \(n = 5, 10, 20\).

Trend Indicators

SMA Crossover

\[\text{SMA}(n)_t = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} C_{t-i}, \qquad \text{Signal} = \frac{\text{SMA}(s)}{\text{SMA}(l)} - 1\]

Motivation: Simple moving averages smooth price noise and reveal trend direction. The ratio of short to long SMA quantifies how much the recent trend deviates from the longer-term baseline.

Interpretation:

  • \(> 0\) bullish (short above long)
  • \(< 0\) bearish; zero-crossing signals a trend change.

Typical pairs: \(s/l = 5/20\), \(10/50\), \(50/200\).

EMA Crossover (Exponential Moving Average)

\[\text{EMA}(n)_t = \alpha\, C_t + (1-\alpha)\,\text{EMA}(n)_{t-1}, \quad \alpha = \tfrac{2}{n+1}\] \[\text{Signal} = \frac{\text{EMA}(s)}{\text{EMA}(l)} - 1\]

Motivation: EMA assigns exponentially decaying weights to past prices, making it more responsive to recent moves than SMA. Reduces lag at the cost of slightly more noise sensitivity.

Interpretation: Same directional logic as SMA crossover but reacts faster; more susceptible to whipsaws in ranging markets.

Typical pairs: \(s/l = 12/26\), \(9/21\), \(5/20\).

MACD Signal Line (Moving Average Convergence/Divergence)

\[\text{MACD}_t = \text{EMA}(s)_t - \text{EMA}(l)_t\] \[\text{Normalised} = \frac{\text{MACD}_t}{C_t}\]

Motivation: Measures the convergence and divergence of two EMAs, revealing momentum shifts before price crossovers occur. Normalising by price makes it comparable across instruments.

Interpretation:

  • Positive and rising = bullish momentum building
  • MACD crosses signal line = entry/exit
  • histogram shrinking = momentum weakening.

Standard parameters: \(s=12\), \(l=26\), signal \(=9\).

ADX — Average Directional Index

\[\text{ADX}(n) = \text{EMA}\!\left(\frac{|{+DI} - ({-DI})|}{+DI + (-DI)},\, n\right) \times 100\]

Motivation: Most trend indicators show direction but not strength. ADX is direction-agnostic — it measures how strongly price is trending regardless of whether up or down.

Interpretation: \(< 20\) ranging; \(20\)–\(25\) developing trend; \(> 25\) strong trend; \(> 40\) very strong. Pair with any crossover for direction. Typical periods: \(n = 7, 14, 21\).

Parabolic SAR

\[\text{SAR}_{t+1} = \text{SAR}_t + \alpha\,(\text{EP} - \text{SAR}_t)\] \[ \alpha_0 = 0.02,\ \alpha_{\max} = 0.20\]

Motivation: A dynamic trailing stop that accelerates as a trend extends. Provides both a binary regime signal (above/below price) and a quantitative distance measure.

Interpretation:

  • Price \(>\) SAR = uptrend
  • Price \(<\) SAR = downtrend; SAR flip = reversal signal.

As a model feature encode as binary or \((C - \text{SAR})/C\).

Momentum Indicators

Rate of Change (ROC)

\[\text{ROC}(n)_t = \ln\!\left(\frac{C_t}{C_{t-n}}\right)\]

Motivation: The simplest and most direct momentum measure — the log-return over a fixed lookback. Captures the cross-sectional momentum anomaly documented extensively in academic literature.

Interpretation: \(> 0\) positive momentum; \(< 0\) negative; magnitude = strength. Use one feature per lookback. Typical periods: \(n = 5, 10, 20, 60\).

RSI — Relative Strength Index

\[\text{RS} = \frac{\overline{\text{Gain}(n)}}{\overline{\text{Loss}(n)}}\ , \, \text{RSI}(n) = 100 - \frac{100}{1 + \text{RS}}\]

Motivation: Compares average up-moves to average down-moves over a window, producing a bounded oscillator \([0, 100]\). Normalises momentum by recent range, making it comparable across volatility environments.

Interpretation:

  • \(> 70\) overbought
  • \(50\) neutral
  • \(< 30\) oversold.

The 50 line acts as a trend filter in many systems. Typical periods: \(n = 7, 14, 21\).

Stochastic Oscillator %K / %D

\[\text{\%K}(n) = \frac{C_t - L_n}{H_n - L_n} \times 100, \, \text{\%D}(m) = \text{SMA}(\text{\%K},\, m)\]

Motivation: Measures where the current close sits within the recent High-Low range. In an uptrend, closes tend toward the top of the range; in a downtrend, toward the bottom.

Interpretation:

  • \(> 80\) overbought
  • \(< 20\) oversold
  • \(\text{\%K}\) crosses \(\text{\%D}\) = entry signal.

Typical params: \(n = 14\), \(m = 3\).

Williams %R

\[\text{\%R}(n) = -100 \times \frac{H_n - C_t}{H_n - L_n}\]

Motivation: The inverse of Stochastic %K, emphasising proximity to the recent high. Mathematically \(\text{\%R} = \text{\%K} - 100\). Highly correlated with Stochastic — in a lean feature set use one or decorrelate.

Interpretation:

  • \(0\) to \(-20\) overbought
  • \(-50\) neutral
  • \(-80\) to \(-100\) oversold.

Typical periods: \(n = 7, 14, 21\).

CCI — Commodity Channel Index

\[\text{TP}_t = \frac{H_t + L_t + C_t}{3}, \qquad \text{CCI}(n) = \frac{\text{TP}_t - \text{SMA}(\text{TP},n)}{0.015 \times \text{MAD}(\text{TP},n)}\]

Motivation: Measures how many mean absolute deviations the typical price is from its moving average. The 0.015 constant places ~70–80% of values in \([-100, +100]\) under normal conditions, combining momentum and mean-reversion signals.

Interpretation:

  • \(> +100\) strong uptrend/overbought
  • \(0\) neutral
  • \(< -100\) strong downtrend/oversold.

Typical periods: \(n = 10, 14, 20\).

Volume Indicators

Volume Log-Change

\[\Delta V_t = \ln\!\left(\frac{V_t}{V_{t-1}}\right)\]

Motivation: Volume is a proxy for market participation and conviction. Log-change normalises for wide variation in absolute volume across instruments and time.

Interpretation:

  • Price up + vol spike = confirmed breakout
  • price up + low vol = weak move
  • price down + vol spike = panic selling.

Divergence between price and volume often precedes reversals.

Volume Ratio

\[\text{VR}(n)_t = \frac{V_t}{\text{SMA}(V,\, n)_t}\]

Motivation: Raw volume is not comparable across instruments or through time. Normalising by rolling average produces a dimensionless activity score indicating whether current participation is above or below its historical norm.

Interpretation:

  • \(\approx 1\) normal
  • \(> 1.5\) above-average interest
  • \(> 2\) unusual/event-driven
  • \(< 0.5\) unusually quiet.

Typical periods: \(n = 10, 20, 50\).

OBV / Rolling Mean

\[\text{OBV}_t = \text{OBV}_{t-1} + \begin{cases} +V_t & C_t > C_{t-1} \\ -V_t & C_t < C_{t-1} \\ 0 & C_t = C_{t-1} \end{cases}\] \[\text{Feature} = \frac{\text{OBV}_t}{\text{SMA}(\text{OBV},\, n)_t}\]

Motivation: Cumulative volume signed by price direction. Rising OBV with flat price indicates accumulation; falling OBV with rising price signals distribution and potential reversal.

Interpretation:

  • OBV rising + price flat = accumulation
  • OBV falling + price flat = distribution
  • OBV confirms trend = high conviction move.

Typical normalisation periods: \(n = 10, 20\).

VWAP

\[\text{VWAP}(n)_t = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=t-n+1}^{t} C_i\, V_i}{\displaystyle\sum_{i=t-n+1}^{t} V_i}\]

Motivation: The volume-weighted average price — the fair value benchmark used by institutional traders. Represents the average price paid per share: both a mean-reversion anchor and an execution quality benchmark.

Interpretation:

  • Price \(>\) VWAP = bullish intraday bias
  • price \(<\) VWAP = bearish
  • crossing VWAP = mean-reversion signal.

Best suited for intraday data; use rolling VWAP for daily bars.

CMF — Chaikin Money Flow

\[\text{MFV}_t = V_t \cdot \frac{(C_t - L_t) - (H_t - C_t)}{H_t - L_t}\] \[\text{CMF}(n) = \frac{\sum \text{MFV}}{\sum V}\]

Motivation: Weights volume by where the close falls within the bar’s range. A close near the high implies buying pressure; near the low implies selling pressure. Summing over a window reveals sustained accumulation or distribution.

Interpretation:

  • \(> +0.25\) strong buying
  • \(0\) to \(+0.25\) moderate accumulation
  • \(-0.25\) to \(0\) distribution
  • \(< -0.25\) strong selling.

Typical periods: \(n = 10, 20, 21\).

Mean-Reversion Indicators

Bollinger Band Position

\[\text{BB}\%(n) = \frac{C_t - \text{SMA}(n)_t}{2\,\sigma_n}\]

Motivation: Defines a dynamic envelope around price based on rolling standard deviation. The position within the bands quantifies how stretched price is relative to recent volatility — the core of mean-reversion strategies.

Interpretation:

  • \(+1\) at upper band (overbought)
  • \(0\) at SMA (neutral)
  • \(-1\) at lower band (oversold).

Band width itself is a volatility regime indicator. Typical periods: \(n = 10, 20, 50\).

Distance from 52-Week High / Low

\[d_{\text{high}}(n) = \frac{C_t - \max(C,\, n)}{C_t}, \qquad d_{\text{low}}(n) = \frac{C_t - \min(C,\, n)}{C_t}\]

Motivation: 52-week highs and lows are psychologically and structurally significant price levels. Breakouts above the 52-week high have strong documented momentum effects; proximity to the low captures distress or value.

Interpretation:

  • Near 52w high = momentum or resistance
  • breaking 52w high = strong breakout signal
  • near 52w low = distress or value opportunity.

Typical periods: \(n = 52, 26, 13\) weeks.

Z-Score of Price

\[Z(n)_t = \frac{C_t - \text{SMA}(n)_t}{\sigma_n}\]

Motivation: Places current price in a standardised statistical context — how many standard deviations from its recent mean. The cleanest, most model-friendly mean-reversion feature, especially useful for pairs trading and stat-arb strategies.

Interpretation:

  • \(> +2\) statistically overbought
  • \(\approx 0\) near mean
  • \(< -2\) statistically oversold.

Near-identical to BB% with the same \(n\) — one may be redundant. Typical periods: \(n = 20, 60, 120\).
